How the colour is applied — and why it stays honest:
The stripe is the channel. A 4px bar on the left edge, not coloured text. Coloured body text at 13–14px is the thing BDA guidance warns about; a stripe carries the signal without touching legibility. Actor colour = side. Blue names are ours, red names are theirs. You can tell whose turn it is before reading a single word.
Every pill has a word. Hit, Critical, Miss, Slain, Spell, Reaction. Turn the monitor to greyscale and nothing is lost — the dashed border on Miss and the filled Slain badge still separate them. Health bars replace bare numbers. 16 / 18 still prints, but the bar shows the proportion instantly. Colour shifts green → amber → red as a second cue, with the fraction always present.
Grey means nothing happened. Misses and collapsed groups are the only grey rows, so the eye skips them on the way to the rows that mattered. Brass means exceptional. Criticals get their own channel rather than being "a stronger red" — in this fight 3 of 4 kills were crits, which the old flat log never let you see.
Round tallies. "1 kill · 10 damage dealt" in the header answers the question most players actually have without reading the rows at all. Per-hero filters. Following one character through a fight is one click, rather than scanning 25 interleaved lines for a name.
The same five channels run through the character sheet. Red is offence in both places, blue is defence in both, grey is "authored but doing nothing" in both — which is how the Wounding rider on Torvald's sword and a missed swing end up looking related, because they are: in each case the game is showing you something that produced no effect.
