The accessory drawer ambience by default · proposed jobs in red

Inkwell & quill

Sits by the rail. Proposed job: the player's pen. Quill rests in the well when nothing awaits you; it lies out, inked, when a choice or order is pending.

Rocker blotter

Lives at the desk's foot. Proposed job: the receipt. After any wax-seal commitment, the order "blots dry" here — the last committed action faintly mirrored on the felt.

Sealing wax & taper

Beside the correspondence. Proposed job: the cost cue. Hovering any seal-red button leans the taper's flame toward it — irreversibility signaled before the click.

Pounce pot

Sand for drying ink. Proposed job: the turn. "Resolve week" is the scribe sanding the ledger — the pot anchors week-advance and hosts its brief resolution shimmer.

Thread spool

Where the red thread comes from. Proposed job: the source. Hovering a quest draws thread from the spool, over its notice, to its X on the chart.

Letter knife

Brass and steel. Proposed job: unread mail. The knife lies across any unopened letter — correspondence waiting is visible across the room.

The stance: accessories are set dressing — they reinforce that this is a desk, and they vanish in flat mode. None of them carries grammar meaning unless we give it a job from the red list above, and any job must keep its number/label explicit elsewhere. Which jobs get hired is the round-03 debate.

Changed since round 02 materials · grammar · chart

  • Materials: tooled + stitched border on the desk edge, a second coarse grain layer on the leather, fiber flecks in the vellum, deckled edges available for the oldest sheets (demonstrated on the open-questions sheet opposite).
  • Grammar: selected notice now shows a red-tinged lift (chosen = thread-connected); empty board slots read as bare leather; marginalia demonstrated on a notice, the ledger, and the chart itself.
  • Chart: denser survey — stipple sea, roads, ruin, marsh, bridge, cartouche, five region labels — and pressure washes now carry red-ink annotations so the color is never unlabeled.
  • Assembly: deskbar (brass plates, explicit numbers), four-slot board, rail of letter-on-blotter / ledger / watch report / tally.

Open questions for this round's debate

  • Which accessory jobs get hired, which stay pure ambience?
  • Chart density: is round 03 the ceiling, or push further (hachured relief shading, more marginalia)?
  • Deckled edges: oldest sheets only (as here), or never?
  • Deskbar: brass plates vs. writing the numbers in ink on the desk ledger itself?
  • The screen composes at ~1140px. Real app is wider — does the chart earn more width, or does the board get a fourth visible notice?

The desk grammar unchanged · the load-bearing vocabulary

brass pin — pinned sheets are things you can act on
tape — standing records, not actionable
V
wax seal — commitment actions (Accept, Launch)
red thread — relates a quest to its place on the chart
URGENT
ink stamp — arc beats, expiring postings
a note in red ink
marginalia — the beat feed lives in the margins

The grammar: every physical affordance carries one consistent meaning. Pins = actionable now. Tape = reference. Wax = irreversible commitment. Red ink = the world talking back. Accessories sit outside the grammar until hired. Fixed status colors (#0ca30c / #fab219 / #ec835a / #d03b3b) stay label-paired everywhere.
