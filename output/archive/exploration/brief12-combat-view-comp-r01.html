Before the comp — what the stream actually contains measured today against the real sim, not inferred

Three things in the handoff turned out not to match the code. None of them touch the two decisions you already took — they change what the view can be built out of, so the comp below is drawn to the measured facts.

The handoff saysThe sim actually doesConsequence for the view
“The sim emits continuous 2D positions.” No. unit_moved has exactly one call site (encounter.ts:365) and fires only on the !arrivedBefore && arrivedAfter edge — the moment a unit enters attack range of its current target. A 50-tick 4v4 emits 9 of them; a whole 4-fight dungeon dispatch emits 23. The field cannot replay recorded motion. It must interpolate between waypoints and say so. See decision D1.
purpose is engage | standoff | flee | reposition — “exactly R3’s failure vocabulary”. Only engage and standoff are ever emitted. flee and reposition appear nowhere in src/. combat.unit_fled is never emitted at all — interpret.ts has a case for a line that cannot occur. “Flee-oscillation” is not unrendered — it is unsimulated. The view can’t show it. Kiting shows up instead as ranged standoff churn, which is visible.
Show “a stalemate forming” before stalemate_forced fires. Stalemate needs 300 ticks (30 s) of damage silence. Measured fight lengths: median 32–91 ticks, P90 143. All four committed harness scenarios report stalemateRate: 0. A bespoke stalemate visual would be art for an event that has never fired. The comp uses a silence gauge instead — one derived number that would catch it, and catches kiting today.

Also confirmed, and unchanged: the stream carries instance ids only — no name, no maxHp, no side metadata. Nothing in it can label a dot or size an HP bar. That is exactly the hole combat.unit_spawned fills, and every unit in the comp below is labelled from a simulated spawn payload.

Two more measurements that shaped the layout both cut against the existing 1× / 4× / 16× transport

Combat is most of the record, and none of the picture. In three real dispatches, combat.* events were 43%, 57% and 82% of the whole stream, and combat occupied 27%, 46% and 79% of the ticks. The current playback screen renders a room graph for the other side of that split and drops all of it into unlabelled beat lines.

16× is not a viewing speed. A whole dispatch at 16× is 1–3 seconds. A median fight at 16× is 0.2 s; at 4×, under a second. The three speeds were sized for travel-and-explore, not for a fight.

So the comp gives combat its own transport — ¼× and ½× at the slow end, and 4×/16× redefined as skim, where the field freezes and the feed carries the fight.

    The five design questions, answered by the comp

    1 · What replaces the dungeon sketch for a surface fight — and do dungeon fights get the field instead of, or beside, the room graph? Beside — one component, two mountings. The graph answers where; the field answers what happened there. On a dungeon run the graph stays exactly as it is, the room in play is ringed in red ink, and the field sits under it (toggle “Beside the room graph” above). A surface fight has no graph, so the field takes its place and the sheet header names the site — the road, the camp. Nothing about the field changes between the two; only its mounting does.
    2 · How does a fight read at 16×? It doesn’t, and pretending otherwise is the lie. Measured: a median fight at 16× is 0.2 s; a whole dispatch is 1–3 s. So the comp splits the transport in two. Watch (¼×, ½×, 1×) animates the field. Skim (4×, 16×) freezes the field to its current state, marks it, and lets the record carry the fight — 16× is the skip control it always really was. Try 16× above: the field stops pretending. Fights are 5–14 s at 1×, so ½× is the honest default for watching, and the comp opens there.
    3 · How do you show a stalemate forming? With the silence gauge, not a bespoke visual. stalemate_forced fires after 300 ticks without damage. Fights end in 32–143. Every committed harness scenario reports stalemateRate: 0. So the gauge simply draws ticks-since-last-wound against that 300-tick window — it is one derived number, it costs nothing, it would catch a stalemate forming if one ever did, and in the meantime it is the kiting read: silence plus motion is exactly the R3 failure. The churn gauge under it counts unit_engaged in the last 5 s, which is the target-thrash tell (it is the second-most-common event in a fight and the feed currently drops it entirely).
    4 · Flat mode — the view is DATA, not ornament. What survives? Everything that is a fact. Flat strips vellum, grain, tilt, pins, and the drawn-by-hand stroke. It keeps the full arena grid, the rule and its tick marks, the scale bar, every unit glyph, every name, every HP number and bar, engagement lines, the silence and churn gauges, the marginalia text, and the whole feed. Side is carried by glyph form (heroes solid, enemies hollow) and by the label, never by colour — so the frozen status set stays label-paired and never load-bearing alone. Toggle it top-right; nothing disappears but the furniture.
    5 · Does the arena need scale and a legend? Yes — and it needs the engagement radius most of all. 1 unit = 5 ft, so the 14×10 arena is 70 × 50 ft. The grid rules every unit and heavies every other; the bottom margin carries a 5-unit scale bar reading “25 ft”. ENGAGEMENT_RANGE = 1.5 u = 7.5 ft is what decides whether a reaction strike can happen at all, so it is drawn as a ring — but only around the selected unit, because eight of them at once is noise. Click any glyph or roster row. Ranged units also get their weapon-range ring, which is what makes a standoff legible as a deliberate distance rather than a unit failing to close.

    What I need you to rule on these are new; the two taken decisions stand untouched

    D1 · Motion: interpolate and admit it, or emit more? My recommendation: interpolate, and put a filenote on the sheet. The field draws each unit from its spawn position, eases toward the next recorded waypoint, and holds. Motion between waypoints is drawn, not recorded — presentation interpreting facts, which is exactly constraint 4. Cost: zero sim change, zero stream growth, no harness diff.
    • The alternative — emit unit_moved on purpose-changes and populate flee/reposition — is a behaviour change, moves every harness snapshot, and roughly doubles fight-stream size (a 4v4 is ~72 events, 9 of them moves). That is R1 territory for a benefit we cannot yet judge, because we have never watched a fight. I'd rather ship the view, watch, and then decide.
    • One small ask if you take the recommendation: add speed to the unit_spawned payload alongside the approved fields. It makes the interpolation derivable rather than guessed, and it is the same additive event you already approved — not a second schema decision.
    D2 · Is “skim mode” right, or should 4×/16× still animate? The comp freezes the field at 4× and 16× and hands the fight to the feed. The alternative is to keep animating and accept that it is illegible. I think freezing is honest and makes 16× useful as the skip it is — but it is a real product call about what watching means, and it is yours.
    D3 · Does the live beat-feed bug get fixed by unit_spawned alone, or do we also cut the noise? unit_spawned carrying name fixes disp_1:camp_e0 → Goblin at the source, for every consumer — that part is settled. The open question: enemies are currently indistinguishable from each other (“Goblin” ×4). The comp disambiguates them as Goblin ɪ, ɪɪ, ɪɪɪ from spawn order, in presentation only, no sim change. Say yes and I'll write it into the brief.
    D4 · Scope check — three things I found that are not brief #12, so I have not designed them in:
    • combat.unit_fled is in the frozen vocabulary and in interpret.ts, but nothing emits it. Dead branch, or unbuilt feature?
    • stalemate_forced.resolution can only ever be 'byState'; 'attackersWithdraw' is unreachable.
    • Reaction strikes are nearly extinct — 1 reaction_triggered across 40 4v4 fights. Units close and swing in the same tick, so almost nothing ever provokes. If the view is meant to make reactions visible, there may be nothing to see.
    All three are combat-model questions, not view questions. I'd log them and keep #12 to the view — confirm and I'll record them in the ledger rather than chase them.
