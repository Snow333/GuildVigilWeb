## 1. The game in one paragraph

You run an adventurers' guild out of a small, run-down town. You create characters, group them into
parties, and send them on missions and quests across a world map you are slowly exploring. Missions
play out as an auto-battler; parties come home successful or not, with a summary of what happened,
plus rewards, experience, items and pieces of the story. You level your characters, gear them, and
grow the town with resources your parties secure. A story that starts ordinary turns elaborate and
diabolical around levels 12–14 and ends far across the map, against a lich and his undead army.

## 2. The rules base

- **Pathfinder 2e is the basis, not a straitjacket.** Where a tabletop rule plays badly in a digital
  auto-battler, change it.
- **Use what the project already defines:** stats, ancestries, classes, skills, spells, feats,
  enemies, items.
- **Level cap is 20** (assumed for now).

## 3. The loop

| Step | What the player does |
|---|---|
| **Build the guild** | Create characters and assign them to parties. |
| **Pick a mission** | Choose missions and quests on a world map that is revealed as you explore. |
| **Watch it play out** | The mission runs as an auto-battler. *(Later: the player can choose to take direct control of a party for a special mission.)* |
| **Read the result** | Parties return successful or unsuccessful, with a summary of what happened. |
| **Reap the rewards** | Experience, items, resources, and story details are revealed. |
| **Level up** | On return, with enough XP. The player picks abilities/feats, spells and skills — **or** presses "auto level up" and the game picks from a class template. |
| **Grow the town** | Spend resources to upgrade parts of the town. |

## 4. Town, guild hall and the world map

- **Resources come from the map.** Parties secure sites — a lumber yard (wood), a mine, a magical
  node, etc. — which then feed the town.
- **Resources upgrade the town.** Each building improves something the player cares about.
- **The guild hall's level sets your party capacity**, for example:

  | Guild hall | Parties |
  |---|---|
  | Start | 1 party of 4 |
  | Upgrade | 1 party of 5 |
  | Upgrade | 1 party of 6 |
  | Upgrade | 2 parties: one of 6, one of 4 |
  | … | and onward |

- **Outposts.** The player can establish outposts so parties operate closer to the action and
  avoid long travel times.

## 5. Story

- Story plots use the **enemies already defined in the project**.
- It **starts very typical** and **unfolds into something elaborate and diabolical** as the
  characters reach **levels 12–14**.
- **The big arc ends against a lich and his undead army, very far off on the world map.**

## 6. What we care about most

This is the quality bar. When time is short, these get it first.

1. **Rich character classes.** A class is rich because of its **abilities and its stat
   progression**. Every class builds toward a flavourful style of play, for example:
   - touch-casting wizard / sorcerer
   - tanky warrior
   - ranged-attack ranger
   - sneak-attack rogue
   - …and so on for every class.
2. **Skills that matter** — Trickery, Survival, Arcana, Religion, etc. — each giving the benefits
   already defined in the project.
3. **Interesting equipment choices** that let the player further define a character.
4. **Interesting enemy types** with strengths and weaknesses the player can **exploit through
   builds and equipment**.
5. **A story that escalates** from ordinary to diabolical (see §5).
